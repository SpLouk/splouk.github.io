I'm currently unemployed. I'm not lazy, I'm waiting for the next big thing.

> In the summer of 1998, I got an opportunity to talk with Jobs again. I said, “Steve, this turnaround at Apple has been impressive. But everything we know about the PC business says that Apple cannot really push beyond a small niche position. The network effects are just too strong to upset the Wintel standard. So what are you trying to do in the longer term? What is the strategy?” He did not attack my argument. He didn’t agree with it, either. He just smiled and said, “I am going to wait for the next big thing.”

― Richard P. Rumelt, Good Strategy Bad Strategy: The Difference and Why It Matters

A [classic research paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF00349198) from 1981, "Time resources and laziness in animals", found that many animals spend a lot of their time doing nothing at all.

The Steve Jobs quote above describes how Jobs managed Apple. In his own personal life he was a workaholic. Still, I think one's career can be approached this way too, and I intend to take this approach for myself. Sometimes, the next big thing (the next job) is not immediately obvious. In these times, I expect the best thing to do (often, not always) is wait. Enjoy the moment. Live for today. Read widely and stay curious. Let the "next big thing" reveal itself in its own time.

## Non-linearity

A thing I did not understand when I was a little younger than I am now is that life is extremely non-linear. The reason I didn't understand this is because it's not made obvious to people living in certain social strata of certain cultures; in fact, you could say this fact is obscured. Many people want to feel that their life has a direction. It's comforting to think that all this toil and effort is _for_ something; that there will be some point in the future at which you will have "arrived" at happiness, fulfillment or whatever. But this is an illusion, and when you give up an illusion, you are free from the burden of maintaining it.
