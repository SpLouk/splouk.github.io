title: "AI Has No Moat"
tags: AI

It has become all the rage to talk about a bubble in AI (really LLM) related investment. Even people who believe the technology is worth every bit its hype have decided that it is ok to acknowledge an AI bubble. Sam Altman himself made comments acknowledging it! The intelligent opinion of those in-the-know now seems to be essentially, "Yes, AI is in a bubble, but it is still a fundamentally revolutionary technology. Just like the dotcom bubble, there will be winners and losers. The next Amazon and Google are out there, but nobody knows who they are yet."

While I definitely agree that LLMs are fundamentally a disruptive and important technology, I'm not so convinced that there will be big winners of this technology like there were from the internet revolution. The simple reason for this is that I can't see an obvious "moat" for any part of the AI stack.

When you look at the internet companies that have become extremely valuable, they all have moats. Every company must have a moat to have high profits long-term, otherwise competing businesses would enter their markets and whittle away at their margins. For internet companies, the moat is usually a network effect. Given that the internet is a network, it makes sense that the most profitable internet companies have a network for their moat. Many internet companies are marketplaces, which have strong network effects. Amazon, Uber, Airbnb are all marketplaces. Facebook, Twitter, etc. are social networks.

My belief that there is no moat in AI rests on the assumption that LLM progress is following an S-curve. In other words, that the [scaling law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neural_scaling_law) will (or in my view, already has) hit diminishing ROI. The cost of each new generation of LLM has increased exponentially, and the availability of new data on which to train models is running out. Meanwhile, newer models seem to be incrementally better than old ones. If AI does not follow an S-curve, and we're somehow in a world where models will continue to advance by leaps and bounds, then the labs producing frontier models may be able to charge high fees for access to these advanced models.

However, it seems to me that models offered by Anthropic, OpenAI, Google, and x.ai are not significantly more capable than one another. What more, open source models and small language models offer competitive alternatives to these frontier models. So lets assume that the AI model landscape is and will continue to be highly competitive.

If the models are interchangeable, the cost of switching is also very low. This is especially true for pro users who pay for LLM services and use them to power apps like Cursor for programming. These apps literally allow a user to swap between models, meaning that customers can freely pick the cheapest model that satisfies their needs.

For users who only know of ChatGPT, they may be less interested in switching. Still, most of these users (>95% of ChatGPT users) do not pay for the product. If ChatGPT's brand recognition could be seen as a moat, then maybe they will be able to create a profitable business out of running this service for free and showing ads to users, which they are planning to roll out soon. It's possible that OpenAI will become a business like Google: a highly profitable ads business built on a free product with superior brand recognition. However, it is much, much more expensive for ChatGPT to respond to user queries than it is for Google to respond to a Google search, so their ads will have to be much more lucrative than Google's for OpenAI to be similarly profitable. Plus, presumably they will need to continue to train new models in order to stay ahead of competition. This isn't impossible, but I'm skeptical.

For the AI "wrappers", Claude Code, Cursor, Perplexity, etc. to be profitable, they would need to be able to charge users for access to these tools.
