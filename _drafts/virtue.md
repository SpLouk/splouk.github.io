<p class="first-paragraph">
Recently I enjoyed listening to <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=N1KHs9hQ1V4">this talk</a> by Dr. Michael Levin. I also recently read and liked C.S. Lewis' "The Abolition of Man". Both this talk by Dr. Levin and this essay by Lewis make an argument for the existence of real things that are not embodied in the rules of physics, chemistry or biology. Dr. Levin makes the claim using evidence from his background in biology. For example, the cicada emerges from its underground life once every 13 or 17 years (depending on the subspecies) to reproduce. The reason it does so is because these are prime numbers; this life cycle pattern makes it challenging for another species to evolve to predate on cicadas. Levin claims that because this behaviour is explainable only by math, not by the laws of physics, we must acknowledge the impact of pure math or the real world. He goes on the make grander claims in this vein later in the talk.
</p>

C.S. Lewis also makes an ambitious claim: that moral virtue, sin and ethics exist actually exist. This is in response to the postmodernist view that morality is arbitrary, or at least explainable in terms of biological instinct or social power structures.
